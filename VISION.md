# Vision

Two apps, one idea: a life leaves a shape in space and in time, and both
are worth keeping. Randhawa holds the shape; Bhullar holds the rhythm.
Memories are the bridge: made in either, shown in both, each app giving
them the dimension the other cannot.

Everything else follows from four refusals: no accounts, no tracking, no
servers, no noise. The apps are quiet because the subject, a person's
life, is loud enough.

## The names

Randhawa and Bhullar are Punjabi clan names, and the apps wear them the
way people do: as inheritance, not branding.

Randhawa, by one old reading, joins ran, the battlefield, with dhava, the
charge: the one who goes first into the field. That is what the space app
does each time it opens. It claims the ground you are standing on with a
single dot, and asks nothing else.

Bhullar's origin is less settled, but in Punjabi the name sits a breath
away from bhulna, to forget. The time app is a long argument with that
sound. Days that hold memories turn gold, and when a date comes back
around, what you wrote returns exactly on time. And notice what the grid
counts: never only what has passed, always what remains.

The crossing is the point. Perhaps the first Randhawa most people can
name is Baba Buddha, and his story is a story about time. The sakhi goes
that as a boy he told Guru Nanak that death does not wait for old age,
since the young crop is cut as readily as the ripe one, and Nanak said
the child spoke like an old man, a buddha. The name held for more than a
century of life. So the best remembered Randhawa is remembered for
understanding time, and the app named like forgetting is built to
remember. Each carries the other inside it, which is exactly what the
memories feature is in code: made in one, shown in the other.

And one wink, labeled as a wink: the first great patron of Guru Nanak,
the landlord who believed before almost anyone and gave land for it, was
Rai Bular. A Bhatti, not a Bhullar; the echo is a coincidence of sound.
But it is a fine coincidence: a name one breath from forgetting,
remembered five hundred years for seeing first.

## The rule

None of this leads. It is never a headline, a splash screen, an About
page essay, or a line in the App Store description. The apps present as
what they are: a map of dots, a grid of dots. The story lives here in the
source, and in a few quiet corners of the code, for whoever cares enough
to look. Discovery is the feature. Anyone who finds it should come away
believing the true thing: that everything in these apps was chosen this
carefully, including the silence about it.

When the apps someday say "open source" and link here, the link says only
that. The story stays where you found it.
