The ink.

Randhawa now draws the map of your life by default. Carry your phone and each time it notices you have gone somewhere, a dot lands and a line joins it to the last one. Home darkens into a blot. The road you take every morning darkens too. Today is drawn on top in orange, the live line over everything before it.

It asks first. The intro says what the map will do, iOS asks in its own words, and iOS asks again before background access becomes permanent. It listens only for the low-power signals a sleeping app is allowed, never runs continuously, and never keeps your phone awake. Answer While Using only and Randhawa marks a dot each time you open it, as it always has. One screen turns it off, one tap forgets everything it gathered.

Tap a blot to open the place: what it is called, how often you were there, what you kept there, and a way to keep something more. Tap a gold dot to open that memory. The camera is in the composer now, next to the library.

The sparkles button still flips between the map and the constellation. Press and slide it, and the map fades under the ink to anywhere in between.

"Export your map" in the menu writes everything to one file you can keep. It is yours.

Where your places live has not changed at all. The same file on your phone, the same optional private iCloud that we cannot read, the same absence of servers of ours.
