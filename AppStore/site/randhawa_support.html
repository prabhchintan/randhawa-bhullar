Support · Randhawa & Bhullar

Two small apps, one idea each. Randhawa marks a dot where you are each time you open it — over time, your places draw a map only you can read. Bhullar is a telescope for time: months, weeks, days, and hours as one grid of dots.

Randhawa FAQ

How does the map grow?

One dot per open. Open the app somewhere and a dot lands there; opening it again within a minute doesn't add another. There is no background tracking — the map only knows the moments you looked.

What are the two views?

Tap the button in the top corner to switch. Map shows your dots over a real map, darkening where you return. Constellation shows just your dots on black — the abstract shape of your places, and the view your widget shows.

How do I add the widget?

Touch and hold an empty area of your Home Screen until the apps jiggle, tap the + in the corner, search for Randhawa, choose a size, and tap Add. The Lock Screen versions live under Customize on the Lock Screen.

How do I erase my map?

Tap the ⋯ button in the app, then "Erase all moments." This deletes the only copy in existence — there is no backup on any server, so it cannot be undone.

Where does my location data go?

Nowhere. It's read only while the app is open, stored in a file on your device, and never transmitted. Read the privacy policy.

What happened to the year of dots?

Randhawa 1.0 showed the year as a grid of dots. That idea lives on — bigger — in our sibling app Bhullar, a telescope for time: months, weeks, days, and hours as dots, with the same widgets.

Bhullar FAQ

How do I change the scale?

Tap anywhere on the dots. Each tap zooms: 12 months → 52 weeks → 365 days → 24 hours, then back around. The app remembers the scale you left it on.

How do I add Bhullar's widgets?

Same as above, but search for Bhullar — there are two widgets, Year in Dots and Day in Dots, so both scales can sit on your Home Screen at once. They update on their own: hourly for the day, at midnight for the year.

Why does today show 23 (or 25) hours sometimes?

Because it really does. On the day the clocks change, Bhullar counts the real hours between midnights — 23 in spring, 25 in fall — the same way a leap year gets 366 dots. The grid never lies.

Does Bhullar collect any data?

No. Bhullar collects nothing, requests no permissions, and makes no network connections at all. See the privacy policy.
